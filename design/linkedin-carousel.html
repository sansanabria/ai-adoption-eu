What counts as sensitive

  • Client & personal dataNames, employers, contact details: anyone identifiable.
  • Account & case numbersAnything that maps back to a specific record.
  • Exact financial figuresReal balances, amounts and dates. Round them or describe them instead.
  • Credentials & internal infoLogins, API keys, and anything not yet public.

If you wouldn't post it on the company intranet, don't paste it into an AI chat tool.

Once it's sent, it can't be pulled back. The problem stays in; the names and numbers come out.

Why AI makes things up

  • No inside viewIt can't see your company's systems, only what you tell it.
  • A cutoff dateA new regulation or policy update might not be in there yet.
  • Fluent, not carefulIt sounds just as sure when it's guessing as when it's right.
  • Too agreeablePush back and it often agrees with you, which doesn't make you right.

One prompt that helps

"Always tell me where this comes from. If you don't have a real source, or you're not sure, say so instead of guessing."

Then check anything factual before you rely on it.

Who owns what AI writes?

Courts have been ruling that purely AI-written work isn't protected by copyright, only the parts where a person made the creative choices.

  • Edit it, don't just copy itReal changes and choices make it yours.
  • Keep a recordNote what was AI-drafted and what you changed.
  • Don't publish it as-isNot under your company's name, if it matters.
  • When unsure, askCheck your company's AI policy or legal team.

Before you automate: is AI in the loop?

No AI step

A scheduled export or a rules engine moving data around. Your normal build, test and sign-off process applies.

AI is one of the steps

If any step reads, summarises, decides or drafts with an AI model, the account rules apply. Test it on made-up data first.
